16:00–17:00 Triangulated polygons and Y-frieze patterns
Jon Wilson (University of Lancashire)
Location: MCS 3070

Abstract: Introduced by Conway and Coxeter in the early 1970s, frieze patterns are arrangements of numbers satisfying a simple local rule. These frieze patterns have since found a natural home in Fomin and Zelevinsky's cluster algebras – a broad algebraic framework intertwining combinatorics, geometry and representation theory. More recently, de Saint Germain developed the notion of Y-frieze patterns as a natural counterpart to Conway–Coxeter friezes. In this talk, based on joint work with my PhD student Hin Chung Henry Tsang, we will classify all Y-frieze patterns of type A_n, settling a conjecture of de Saint Germain that every such pattern arises from a Conway–Coxeter frieze pattern. Furthermore, this classification naturally leads to an enumeration of Y-frieze patterns in terms of Fuss–Catalan numbers.
